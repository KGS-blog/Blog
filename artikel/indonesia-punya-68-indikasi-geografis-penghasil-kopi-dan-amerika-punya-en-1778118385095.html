Indonesia Has 68 Coffee Geographical Indications — And America Has 3 Distinct Taste Zones. It's a Perfect Match, Bro.

As part of data collection for US coffee market research, I've visited more than half of the coffee shops and roasteries in the DMV area (DC, Maryland, Virginia).

Aside from Dua DC in Washington DC, which does serve Indonesian specialty coffee, most others rarely list Indonesian coffee on their menu — especially their pour over menu. I only found 2 coffee shops that offer pour over. Why? Because one barista making a pour over is equivalent to making 5–6 espresso-based drinks in the same amount of time. So either customers are willing to pay significantly more, or it simply gets removed from the menu.

"Java in America is slang for coffee. A cup of java simply means a cup of coffee."

Javanation, Rockville MD: Indonesian Vibes, But Not Java Coffee

When I visited Javanation, a coffee shop in Rockville MD, it's rare to find a shop around here with vibes like back home in Indonesia — outdoor seating with hanging lights everywhere, totally Instagramable. Don't assume the name "Javanation" means it's owned by Javanese people, or that they sell coffee from Java.

On their pour over menu (which is very rarely offered in DMV area coffee shops), there were only Ethiopia, Honduras, and Peru. Meanwhile on their bag display, Sumatra only appeared as a blend component for Javanation's signature blend, alongside Ethiopia. But what made me oddly proud — for the first time I saw "Indonesia" written behind the Sumatra label... I got a little emotional, haha.

"Sumatra coffee works great as a base because of its strong character — both in body and taste notes — making it suitable to blend with just about any other coffee."

— Barista in DMV Area

Sumatra in the American Market: A Strong Base, Not a Single Origin

Here in the DMV East Coast area, Sumatra coffee is well-known as a great blending coffee, not as something familiar standing alone as a single origin. Why? According to one barista, Sumatra coffee works great as a base because of its strong character — both in body and taste notes — making it suitable to blend with just about any other coffee.

Sumatra Mandheling is the champion in the US market. From major traders (Royal New York, Red Goni, Cafe Imports, Genuine Origin, etc.), to retail (Trader Joe's, Walmart, etc.), all the way to cafes and roasteries.

From my observations, Sumatra Mandheling is a market name for Sumatra coffee, not specifically from the Mandheling region. So don't be surprised when you find a Sumatra Mandheling brand that actually comes from Aceh, or even South Sumatra. Same goes for Java Blue, Java Batavia, or Java Sunda — these are all market names. It's a different story for the specialty market, which usually refers to a specific region, but the specialty market is still small despite year-over-year progress.

💡 Interesting Fact: Market Name vs Region Name

Sumatra Mandheling, Java Blue, Java Batavia, Java Sunda — all of these are market names, not specific region names. Traders actually provide clear descriptions, but this market name has stuck with retail buyers. Basically, it's Mandheling coffee regardless of where it actually comes from.

Indonesian Coffee Pricing in the US Market: Commercial vs Specialty

Price-wise, commercial grade is set around $5–$6 per lb (or roughly $11–$13 per kg) for commercial green beans. For specialty, green bean prices can be double or more. For roasted, prices jump again to roughly double — where commercial roasted runs about $22–$26 per kg and specialty roasted $40+ per kg.

Recent data from SpecialtyCoffee.id (January 2026) shows Sumatra Mandheling green beans selling at $17.69–$21.40 per kg, while roasted Mandheling is $33.23–$35.23 per kg. Bulk grade Mandheling Grade 1 is around $8.10 per kg FOB. Global arabica C-prices hit $4.40/lb in 2025, though they've since dropped to around $2.84/lb as of May 2026.

"Indonesian coffee is relatively more expensive. So to lower production costs, blending with cheaper coffee like Brazil and Colombia is done."

Why Is Indonesian Coffee Rarely Used as Specialty?

There are several obstacles why this coffee is rarely used as specialty:

  1. Market name vs consistency. As mentioned before, most Mandheling here is a market brand, not a regional name. So taste consistency often changes even though the character remains similar. Traders actually provide clear descriptions, but this market name has stuck with retail buyers. Basically, it's Mandheling coffee regardless of where it actually comes from.
  2. Relatively higher price. Indonesian coffee is relatively more expensive compared to Brazil or Colombia. So to lower production costs, blending with cheaper coffee is done. Some use Ethiopia as a blend component to increase complexity.
  3. Sumatra's base character. Sumatra coffee plays an important role matching the "American consumer palate," but to achieve a more balanced taste, blending is done by roasteries.

Why Sumatra Mandheling Is So Popular Here

And the other most prominent reasons why Sumatra Mandheling is so popular:

  1. Sustainable supply from Indonesia is one of the supporting factors why Sumatra coffee is quite significant in the US. It's time for other regions to learn from Sumatra.
  2. Roasteries as marketers. The market heavily depends on roasteries' understanding of coffee. Roasteries look at quality and availability, and only Sumatra Mandheling has given roasteries the confidence to market it. Availability issues are the main obstacle for coffee from other regions to develop.

3 American Taste Zones, 68 Indonesian Geographical Indications — A Perfect Pairing

Okay, from previous parts I keep saying "American consumer taste" or "American palate." But what exactly is the American taste preference for coffee?

Well, America is huge, bro! For example, flying from Washington DC to LA California takes 5 hours direct. So you can't generalize consumer preferences. But according to research here and there, reinforced by data from the NCA (National Coffee Association) — specifically the NCDT (National Coffee Data Trends) Spring 2026, which has been running for over 70 years with 1,850+ respondents — coffee drinkers in the US can be divided into 3 regional taste profiles:

Region Characteristics Coffee Preference
Northeast
(New York, Massachusetts, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Maryland, etc.)		 Highest per capita coffee consumption — top 10 coffee drinking states are from this area. Daily consumption at 75%. Influenced by cold weather, culture, and demographics. Tends toward bold and dark roasted.
Southern
(Texas, Florida, etc.)		 Warmer weather, more heterogeneous immigrant population. Iced coffee (including cold brew), artisan coffee, and premium coffee are consumed more.
West Coast
(Washington, Seattle, California)		 Highest specialty coffee consumption (58% past-week). Fastest growing market. Driven by Third Wave Culture — demands high-quality (specialty) coffee and brewing methods have grown significantly.

"Now here's the interesting part: Indonesia has 68 Coffee Geographical Indications with incredibly diverse character — from Aceh Gayo that's earthy-full body, Toraja that's spicy-complex, Flores that's floral-fruity, Papua that's wild-herbal, to Java that's clean-balanced. Meanwhile America has 3 distinct taste zones. Meaning? There's no reason Indonesian coffee can't enter every area in the US."

Northeast wants bold dark roast? Sumatra Mandheling or Aceh Gayo are ready for battle. Southern loves iced and premium? Flores or Bali Kintamani with their fruity notes are perfect for cold brew. West Coast craves specialty with traceability? Toraja or Papua with their unique profiles can be the ace in the hole.

Brewing Methods & Consumption Habits

However, in general, American consumers are among the biggest coffee drinkers in the world. And the main reason for drinking coffee is functional — to boost energy and stay awake. Specialty coffee contributes to taste preference as the third reason consumers drink coffee.

Coffee drip is very popular — dominating 37% of home brewing methods (NCDT 2026). 95% of coffee drinkers start their morning routine with coffee. It's slightly different in urban areas where brewing methods are more varied, though drip coffee still dominates.

"Specialty coffee itself keeps rising — 58% of American adults drank specialty coffee in the past week (up from 53% in 2022). Espresso-based beverages are the main driver: latte 21%, espresso 20%, cappuccino 17%."

And the very significant difference with consumers in Indonesia: instant coffee is at the bottom of the preference list, after drip coffee, single cup, and cold brew. So you'll very rarely find instant coffee in supermarkets — it loses to packaged whole bean coffee. This is also influenced by the consumer culture here that highly prioritizes quality.

Conclusion: Want to Sell Indonesian Coffee in the US? It Depends on Your Target

So, things are becoming clearer. If you want to sell Indonesian coffee in the USA, what kind of coffee should you sell? It depends on where you're selling — don't miss your target.
